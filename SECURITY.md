# Security policy

Receptum handles payments and escrow.

## Reporting

Please **do not** open a public issue. Report privately via GitHub's
[private vulnerability reporting](https://github.com/Pez1980/receptum/security/advisories/new).
We aim to acknowledge reports within 3 business days.

## Scope

- `ReceptumEscrow` (EVM and Soroban) and the chain adapters (fund loss, unauthorized release or refund)
- Receipt construction, signing and verification (forged or mismatched receipts)
- Payment verification in `server` / `client` (paying less than quoted, replay, receipt substitution)

## Status

All contracts and adapters are **unaudited and testnet-only**. Mainnet support will not ship before an independent audit is published here.

## Seller ↔ payout account binding (added after the review, 3 Oct 2026)

Previously a receipt named both the seller's `did:key` and `payment.payee`, but nothing proved one party controls both — a seller could claim another account's payments as its own deliveries. **Account bindings** (SPEC §4.1) close this: a `receptum/account-binding/1` statement signed by the `did:key` **and** by the payout account's chain key (EIP-191 on EVM, ripple-keypairs on XRPL, SEP-53 on Stellar), carried in `SignedReceipt.bindings` outside the hashed receipt. `receptum-verify` reports it as level 2.5 and no longer calls a receipt with a payee `VERIFIED` without one (`--allow-unbound` for legacy receipts); `@receptum/client` and `@receptum/mcp` take `requireBinding`; `@receptum/server` can refuse to charge when its bindings don't cover `payTo`. Limits: no revocation besides `expiresAt`; XRPL regular/master-key state is checked against the account's _current_ `account_info`; Stellar bindings prove the master key signed but don't inspect signers/thresholds; EVM contract wallets (ERC-1271) are unsupported (fail closed). Unreviewed by the independent reviewer so far.

## SPEC alignment with the independent Python verifier (3 Oct 2026)

Writing a second verifier from the spec alone (`verifiers/python`) exposed places where SPEC.md was ambiguous and the two implementations disagreed. The spec now pins each one and both verifiers implement it, with regression tests and negative vectors (`spec/vectors/rrf-v1.json` `invalid`) on both sides. Security-relevant changes to `@receptum/verify`:

- **VERIFIED is stricter** (SPEC §6): it now also requires the delivered file (L1) and an on-chain commitment of `receiptHash` — the escrow rail's own, or a mined anchor for x402. Previously a receipt with no file and no anchor could be VERIFIED. Checks that cannot be performed (RPC/network errors, unsupported rails or networks, an XRPL `account_info` that is not from a validated ledger, an RPC serving another chain) are `unavailable` — never a pass, never a failure. Both CLIs list the missing pieces.
- **Input and envelope** (SPEC §4, §6.1): strict I-JSON input (item 11), exact envelope and `proof` members, duplicate-free JWS headers, `S < L`, and a `did:key` seller for JWS proofs.
- **Rail rules** (SPEC §7): only `x402:exact` is recognised; on EVM, `payment.asset` must be the token contract and the RPC chain id must match; `anchor:evm` anchors must be zero-value, with block numbers reported. Anyone may send an anchor: it proves existence-by-time, not authorship.
- **Validation**: DIDs are never CAIP-10 accounts (a DID payer/payee is rejected), W3C DID percent-encoding, `acceptance.evaluator` only in evaluator mode, binding timestamps compared at full precision, XRPL secp256k1 binding signatures must be canonical DER and low-s. The Python verifier now requires `remedy.kind`, limits fractions to 9 digits, and no longer lets `$` accept a trailing newline.
- **Documented limits** (SPEC §10): no finality guarantee (block/ledger numbers are reported); no required order between settlement and `deliveredAt`; one payment reference may back several receipts — consumers must de-duplicate by payment reference.

**Still open before a final audit:** item 3 for the claimable-balance rail (use the Soroban escrow instead); an audit of the EVM and Soroban `ReceptumEscrow` contracts (the Soroban contract is new in M6 and not covered by the review passes above); an independent review of the account-binding design; binding revocation; and a deployment-provenance check stronger than a published registry (e.g. a factory).
